Plumbing Tape on a Shower Head in Lemon Grove, CA

A shower head that drips at the arm usually needs fresh tape and a gentle hand. Here is how to do it without scratching the finish or cracking the fitting.

Plumbing tape, also called thread seal tape or Teflon tape, is a thin white film that fills the gap between threads on a pipe. When a shower head leaks where it screws onto the shower arm, fresh tape and a snug fit often stop it. It is a simple fix that a homeowner can do in ten minutes with a few tools.

The tape does not hold the joint together. The threads do that. The tape fills the tiny spaces between threads so water cannot squeeze through. That is why wrapping it correctly matters more than using a lot of it.

What you need

  • A new roll of plumbing tape, white PTFE tape for water lines.
  • An adjustable wrench or channel-lock pliers.
  • A clean rag to protect the finish.
  • A small brush or old toothbrush to clean the threads.
  • A step stool.

Step by step

1

Unscrew the old shower head

Turn it counterclockwise. If it sticks, wrap the rag around it and use the wrench gently.

2

Clean the threads

Remove old tape and mineral crust from the shower arm with the brush. Clean threads seal better.

3

Wrap the tape

Hold the arm so the threads face you. Start at the second thread and wrap clockwise, three or four turns, overlapping slightly.

4

Screw on the head

Hand tighten, then a quarter turn with the wrench, wrapped in a rag. Over-tightening can crack plastic.

Which direction to wrap

Wrap in the direction that the fitting turns when you screw it on. Looking at the end of the pipe, that is clockwise. If you wrap the other way, the tape bunches up and tears as you tighten.

How much tape is enough

Three to four wraps suit most shower arms. Too little leaves gaps, and too much stops the head from seating and can leave a bead of tape inside the water path. If tape pokes out below the fitting, trim it with a fingernail or a razor blade.

Plastic shower heads need extra gentleness. Metal arms with a plastic head are common, and the plastic threads can crack with a wrench. Tighten by hand and give only a small extra turn.

If water appears on a ceiling below or inside the wall, stop using the shower and call. A leak at the arm elbow behind the tile needs a plumber who can open the wall.

Hard water and shower heads

White crust on the head is mineral scale. Soak the head in vinegar for an hour to loosen it. Scale blocks the nozzles and makes spray uneven, but it does not by itself cause leaks at the threads.

If scale returns quickly on every fixture, test your water. A softener or filter may be worth considering, and the page on water softeners and filtration explains how to start.

Other fixes when the tape does not stop the drip

If a fresh wrap and a snug fit still leave a drip, look at the shower head itself. Most heads contain a small rubber washer inside the connector. When it hardens or goes missing, water slips past the threads even though the tape is fine. Pop the old washer out with a fingernail and fit a new one of the same size.

A bent or damaged shower arm can also leak. Look for hairline cracks around the threads and for a wobble where the arm meets the wall. If the arm moves, the leak may be at the elbow behind the tile, which is a plumber's job.

Tape or paste: which is better here

Plumbing tape and pipe compound for shower arms
ProductStrengthWeakness
PTFE tapeClean, easy to remove, fine for shower armsNeeds correct wrapping direction
Pipe joint compoundFills larger gapsMessy, and can leave residue in the water path
Both togetherRarely neededCan make threads hard to seat

For a shower head, tape alone is the standard choice. Save pipe compound for metal connections that need a heavier seal, and use the product made for the fitting you have.

Protect the finish while you work

Chrome and brushed finishes scratch easily. Wrap the arm and the head collar in a cloth before you grip them with pliers, and use a smooth-jaw wrench if you have one. If a fixture has a plastic collar, gentle hand tightening is all it needs.

Codes and licensing to know about

Plumbing in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Whether a project like this needs a permit depends on the scope, so ask the City of Lemon Grove building department before you start. Contractors hold a C-36 plumbing license from the Contractors State License Board, which you can verify at cslb.ca.gov.

Plumbing in Lemon Grove: local notes

Helix Water District supplies water to Lemon Grove, and it publishes an annual water quality report. We do not state a hardness figure here, since the report is the source. If you see crust on your shower head within weeks of cleaning it, the report or a home test kit tells you more than the head does.

Nearly half of Lemon Grove homes are rented, based on 2020 Census figures. If your rental shower leaks behind the wall, tell the landlord in writing and do not try to open the wall yourself. A tape fix at the head is something a tenant can do, but a wall leak belongs to the owner and a plumber.
