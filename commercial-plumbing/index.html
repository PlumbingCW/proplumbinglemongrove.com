Commercial plumbing is the plumbing of buildings used for business, such as restaurants, shops, offices, clinics and multi-unit properties. These systems are larger, run more fixtures and take more wear than a home system. A failure often means lost sales or a closed door, so commercial calls are treated as urgent. Our 24/7 line takes after-hours commercial calls, and same-day visits are scheduled when a plumber is available.

We repair leaks, clear drains and main lines, fix toilets, urinals and faucets, and service water heaters in commercial settings.

When a business plumbing problem is an emergency

A backed-up restroom, a flooding kitchen or a broken water line at close of business all qualify. Shut off the water to the affected fixture or the building, move stock and equipment out of the way and call.

If the building is a multi-tenant space, tell the property manager. A leak in one unit can reach several others, and a written report protects every tenant.

For the shops, offices and apartment buildings around Broadway and Main Street, see our Downtown Village page.

Business owner checklist

  • Find the main shutoff and tell staff where it is.
  • Stop use of affected restrooms and sinks.
  • Keep customers and staff away from wet floors and electrical areas.
  • Photograph the problem for your property manager and insurer.

Common commercial plumbing problems

Grease line clogs

Restaurant kitchens send fat into the drain. Regular jetting prevents backups.

Restroom fixture failures

Toilets, urinals and faucets in public restrooms see heavy use and wear fast.

Water heater failure

A commercial heater that fails stops dishwashing and handwashing for the day.

Floor drain backups

Drains in kitchens and back rooms clog with debris and show the problem first.

Lemon Grove notes

Broadway is Lemon Grove's main commercial road, and Lemon Grove Plaza and Country Plaza sit along it. Retail and food businesses along corridors like this share building systems, so one tenant's problem can become the whole building's problem.

The City of Lemon Grove has its own business and permit offices. Ask them which permits apply before any tenant improvement that moves plumbing. We schedule our work around their requirements.

Codes, permits and licensing

For commercial plumbing, the standard to ask about is the California Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Permit needs vary by job, and the local building department is the place to confirm them.

Plumbing contractors in California hold a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can check any license at cslb.ca.gov.

What to do before the plumber arrives

  • Shut off the water at the main or at the affected fixture.
  • Move stock, paper records and electronics away from any water.
  • Keep staff and customers out of wet areas and away from outlets.
  • Note the time the problem started and which fixtures are affected.
