A failing sewer line is an emergency when wastewater comes back into the house. Stop using water, do not flush and call our 24/7 line. We schedule same-day and after-hours visits when a plumber is available, and we confirm what is wrong with a camera before we propose a repair.

Sewer line repair means fixing a broken, collapsed or root-damaged pipe between the house and the connection in the street. The fix may be a short dig-and-replace, a liner pulled through the old pipe, or a full replacement.

Sewer backup: what to do first

1

Stop water use

No sinks, showers, laundry or flushing. Every gallon that goes down adds to the backup.

2

Stay out of the water

Sewage carries bacteria. Keep children, pets and bare skin away from it.

3

Switch off nearby power

If a basement or low area is wet, leave the breaker off until it is dry.

4

Call and photograph

Call us and take photos for your insurer or landlord before any cleanup.

Why sewer lines fail in Lemon Grove

Lemon Grove grew from a lemon-growing town into a suburb over the 20th century, and the sewer laterals under older streets match their age. Clay and cast iron pipes crack at joints, and roots find the cracks.

Roots enter a line through a joint no wider than a hairline, then grow into a mat that catches paper. Each clearing buys months, not years. A camera reveals whether the line is root-bound but intact or cracked and sinking.

The sewer lateral on private property is generally the owner's responsibility up to the connection with the public main. Ask the City of Lemon Grove public works office or your sewer provider where that point sits for your address.

Signs of sewer line damage

Backups in several fixtures

A tub that gurgles or fills when the washer drains points to the main line.

Wet or sunken yard patch

Persistent damp ground or a dip in the lawn along the pipe path.

Sewer smell outdoors

Odor near the cleanout or the yard suggests a break.

Recurring clogs

If a cleaning only lasts weeks, the pipe itself is the problem.

How we diagnose and repair

1

Camera inspection

A camera on a flexible cable travels the line and records cracks, bellies and root intrusion.

2

Locate the defect

A locator at the surface tells us the depth and exact position of the damaged pipe.

3

Choose the repair

Options are spot repair, pipe lining or full replacement, and we explain the tradeoffs.

4

Test and confirm

A final camera pass or water test confirms the line is sound.

Codes, permits and licensing

For sewer line repair, the standard to ask about is the California Plumbing Code, which builds on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Permit needs vary by job, and the local building department is the place to confirm them.

Plumbing contractors in California hold a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can check any license at cslb.ca.gov.
