The City of Lemon Grove's General Plan refers to the established Central Avenue neighborhood, a residential area that lies within walking distance of downtown. We use the name the same way, as a local label for the streets around Central Avenue and not as an official boundary.

This page is written for homeowners and small landlords there. It is about practical plumbing decisions, such as when to repair, when to replace and what to ask before a plumber starts.

Where the Central Avenue area is

Central Avenue forms the southern edge of the city's Downtown Village planning area, so the neighborhood begins just south of downtown. The General Plan names Central Avenue, San Miguel Avenue and Kempf Street as neighborhood streets that residents walk, and notes that sidewalks are not currently provided on them.

The city has not published a neighborhood boundary that we could find. We therefore describe the area by those anchor streets and the trolley line, and we ask for your address on every call.

What the city says about the housing

The General Plan describes the established neighborhoods south of Broadway as mostly single-family homes from the post-World War II boom, with some condominiums and apartments. It also notes that some early estate and farm houses remain.

The plan's Eastern Central Avenue residential area allows more homes per lot than the older pattern, and the plan notes that some apartments and condominiums have been built there in recent years. Citywide, about 47 percent of housing units were rented in 2020, so many calls come from tenants and landlords.

Practical plumbing questions for established homes

Repair or replace

Ask your plumber what the failed part is, how old it is and what the rest of the line looks like. A clear answer helps you decide.

Slow drains that return

If a drain clogs again within weeks, ask for a camera look at the line instead of another quick clearing.

Water heater age

A tank heater that is past ten years deserves a plan. Replace it on your schedule and not at midnight.

Know your shutoff

Find the main water valve before you need it, and make sure it turns. Tenants should know where it is too.

Utilities and nearby areas

Plumbing work in California follows the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), and a plumbing contractor holds a C-36 license that you can check at cslb.ca.gov.
