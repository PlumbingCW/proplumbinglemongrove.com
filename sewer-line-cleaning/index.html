The main sewer line carries everything from your house to the street connection. When it clogs, the lowest drain in the house, often a tub or floor drain, shows it first. Stop all water use and call our 24/7 line. Same-day and after-hours visits are scheduled when a plumber is available.

Sewer line cleaning opens that main line, usually through a cleanout, with a motorized cable and a cutter head, followed by flushing and a camera check when the cause is unclear.

Sewer backup: what to do while you wait

  • Stop using sinks, showers and the washing machine.
  • Do not flush any toilet.
  • Keep people and pets away from wet floors and drains.
  • Find your cleanout, a capped pipe near the house or the property line, and clear the area around it.

Do not open a cleanout cap yourself if sewage is pushing at it. The pressure can spray wastewater. We can do it safely with the right equipment.

Why main lines clog

Roots

Roots enter at joints, grow into mats and snag paper and wipes.

Grease

Kitchen fat hardens in the pipe and narrows it year after year.

Wipes and debris

Flushable wipes do not break down the way paper does.

Belly in the pipe

A low spot holds water and solids. Each cleaning only delays the next clog.

Lemon Grove notes

Lemon Grove covers 3.88 square miles, and its older blocks developed over many decades, so house sewers vary in age and material from one street to the next. The cleanout location depends on the house. Look near the foundation and along the property line.

If you rent, tell the landlord in writing and keep records. Nearly half of Lemon Grove homes are renter-occupied, and responsibility for a backup depends on the lease and on whether the blockage is in the owner's line.

Codes, permits and licensing

Work on sewer line cleaning in California is governed by the California Plumbing Code, a state version of the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Whether a permit applies depends on the scope, so ask the City of Lemon Grove building department or ask us before work begins.

Plumbing contractors in California hold a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can check any license at cslb.ca.gov.
