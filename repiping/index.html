Repiping in Lemon Grove, CA

When pipes keep leaking, patches stop making sense. A repipe replaces the supply lines through the house so the leaks stop for good.

Repiping is the replacement of a home's water supply lines, either throughout the house or in sections, with new pipe such as PEX or copper. It does not touch the drain and sewer lines. The goal is to end repeated leaks, bring back water pressure and clear water that has turned brown or metallic.

A good repiping plan starts by finding out what is in the walls today and how many leaks have shown up. One leak in sound pipe is a repair. Several leaks in a short time point to pipe that is wearing out everywhere.

Signs it is time to repipe

Repeated leaks

Two or three leaks in a year in different places mean the whole system is aging.

Brown or rusty water

Galvanized steel rusts from inside and delivers that rust at the tap.

Low pressure

Corrosion narrows old steel pipe until flow drops at every fixture.

Pinholes in copper

Tiny leaks along a copper run suggest thin walls or aggressive water.

If a pipe has burst and water is spraying, that is an emergency. Call our burst pipe repair line first. We can discuss repiping after the water is stopped.

How a repipe works

1

Survey the house

We walk the plumbing, count fixtures and map the routes.

2

Plan access

Most routes go through attics, crawl spaces and closets to limit wall openings.

3

Replace the lines

New pipe is run to every fixture and connected to a manifold or main.

4

Test and restore

The system is pressure tested, then small wall openings are patched.

Disruption and timeline

Most repipes take a few days depending on the house. Water is off for part of each day. We tell you in advance which days and for how long, so you can plan around it.

Wall openings are small and strategic. We patch drywall to a paintable state, but finishing and painting are separate trades.

Lemon Grove notes

Lemon Grove has been a settled community since the 1890s, and its housing was built across many decades. The age of the house gives a clue to the pipe. Older homes may have galvanized steel. Mid-century homes often have copper. Newer homes use PEX or CPVC.

Helix Water District supplies the water. We do not state a hardness figure. If you want to understand how your water interacts with copper, ask Helix for its annual water quality report.

You get a written price before work begins. Insurance rarely covers repiping for age, but it may help when a leak caused water damage.

Codes, permits and licensing

The California Plumbing Code, based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC), sets the rules behind repiping. Your plumber should work to that code, and you can ask which parts of your job need a permit or inspection.

Plumbing contractors in California hold a C-36 license from the Contractors State License Board, and you can check any license at cslb.ca.gov.

What to do if a pipe fails before the job

  • Close the main water valve to stop the flow.
  • Turn off the water heater if the leak is on the hot side.
  • Open a low faucet to drain the line and relieve pressure.
  • Keep electrical devices away from wet walls and floors.
