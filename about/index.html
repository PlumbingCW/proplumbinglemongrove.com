Lemon Grove Pro Plumbing is a call-based plumbing service for Lemon Grove, California, and the East County communities around it. We take calls 24 hours a day and dispatch licensed, insured plumbers to the job. We do not publish individual plumber names or license numbers here, because jobs are dispatched to whichever plumber is available.

What you can verify is the plumber who arrives. Any contractor in California holds a license you can look up on the Contractors State License Board website at cslb.ca.gov. The plumbing classification is C-36, and the work itself falls under the California Plumbing Code, which is based on the Uniform Plumbing Code (UPC). Ask for the license number at the door, and check it.

Where we work

Our address is 7220 Broadway, Lemon Grove, CA 91945. Broadway is one of the city's main roads, and the World's Biggest Lemon stands at Broadway and Lemon Grove Avenue. That giant lemon began as a parade float in 1928 and was made a permanent sculpture in 1930.

Lemon Grove took its name from the groves that covered the area after the community was subdivided in 1892. By 1894 a San Diego newspaper called it a sea of lemon trees. The orchards are long gone, and the city is now fully built out, so most plumbing work here means older pipes inside established homes.

What we do not do

We do not take form submissions. Every request starts with a phone call. We do not claim specific response times. We do not publish reviews, ratings or customer counts that we cannot show. We do not post prices on this site that we cannot honor on your particular job.

We also stay within the work we describe on our service pages. If a job needs a trade we do not provide, such as drywall finishing or electrical work, we tell you and point you to the right trade.

The homes we are called to

Lemon Grove grew from 19,348 residents in 1960 to 27,627 in 2020, and the 2020 count included 9,345 housing units. Roughly 53 percent were owner-occupied and 47 percent were rented. That mix shapes the calls we take: homeowners deciding whether to repair or replace, and tenants and landlords who need a leak or a dead water heater handled quickly.

Because much of the housing was built over several decades, one street can hold pipes from different eras. A plumber who arrives should expect to find mixed materials, and should tell you which parts are sound and which are near the end of their life.

How to check any plumber, including ours

Before work begins, ask for the contractor license number and look it up at cslb.ca.gov. A plumbing contractor holds a C-36 classification. The page shows whether the license is active and whether the contractor has a bond on file.

Ask who is responsible for the work, what the written scope is and when you will be told about any change. A reliable plumber answers these without hesitation.

Why we publish guides

Our blog explains common plumbing problems in plain language, from how a main water shutoff valve works to what a leak detection visit costs. The goal is to help you decide what to do before you call, and to recognize when a problem is small enough to wait and when it is not.

We write these guides for homeowners and landlords, and we mark where a number depends on your house instead of quoting a figure we cannot support. If a guide leaves your question open, call and ask.
